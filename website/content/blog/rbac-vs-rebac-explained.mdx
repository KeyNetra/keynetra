# RBAC vs ReBAC explained

RBAC and ReBAC solve different problems. Treating them as substitutes usually produces awkward systems.

## RBAC is stable when job function matters

Roles work well when permissions align with durable organizational responsibilities. Finance admins and support leads are clear examples.

## ReBAC is natural when collaboration matters

When access depends on ownership, delegation, or shared resources, relationships map the product better than roles do. A user can be a viewer on one document, an editor on another, and a maintainer for a workspace.

## Use both deliberately

Most applications benefit from roles for the baseline and relationships for the exceptions that are not really exceptions at all, but core product behavior.
