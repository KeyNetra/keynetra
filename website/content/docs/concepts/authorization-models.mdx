# Authorization models

Most real systems use more than one authorization model. KeyNetra is designed around that reality.

## RBAC

Role-Based Access Control maps stable permissions to roles such as `admin`, `support`, or `billing_manager`. It works well when access is shaped by job function.

## ReBAC

Relationship-Based Access Control models graph edges like `owner`, `editor`, `viewer`, or `member_of`. It excels when access depends on who is connected to what.

## ACL

Access Control Lists attach explicit grants to individual resources. They are useful for direct sharing and one-off overrides.

## Policy engine

Policies evaluate runtime context such as tenant, environment, attributes, or request metadata. This handles conditional logic that should not be encoded into roles alone.

## Compose instead of choosing one forever

The strongest pattern is usually:

- RBAC for baseline product permissions
- ReBAC for collaboration and delegation
- ACL for direct grants
- Policy rules for constraints and exceptions
